Aeaea
Adapted from Books 10, 11, and 12 of Homer's Odyssey, told in the oral tradition of the 8th century BC. Circe's island Aeaea is placed by ancient sources near the Laestrygonian shore, later identified with Monte Circeo on Italy's Tyrrhenian coast. The Nekyia of Book 11 is the oldest extended description of the Greek underworld. Elpenor, Eurylochus, and the named crew are Homer's own; the lines that history did not record are imagined from what the poem implies. Figure scale: each crew figure represents one companion. Six of twelve survive to Aeaea; one more is lost here. The catalogue does not look away from what commands cost, or who pays them.
